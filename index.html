Proposed Utility Rate Adjustments for the City of Watsonville

Introduction

When you turn on the tap, flush the toilet, or put your bins on the curb, Watsonville’s Public Works and Utilities teams ensure everything works perfectly. Our dedicated staff is on call 24 hours a day. To protect our environment and maintain these reliable services, the City is proposing adjustments to our utility rates.

Critical Factors

💧 Valuable Infrastructure

Valuable Infrastructure
  • Water: We maintain 178 miles of pipes, 6 tanks, 12 wells, and a treatment plant that serves more than 65,000 people.
  • Sewer: We treat 5.5 million gallons of sewage daily through 170 miles of pipes.
  • Trash: 52 employees run the drop-off center and sweep 10,000 miles of streets yearly.

⏳ Why Now?

Why Now?
  • Failing to replace the wastewater plant now will force the City to pay top dollar for emergency repairs when equipment last updated nearly 40 years ago breaks down.
  • Inflation has sharply increased costs for chemicals, fuel, and pipes.
  • A sewer project estimated at $15M in 2021 now costs $37M. Waiting guarantees higher costs.

💵 Transparent and Cost-Based Rates

Transparent and Cost-Based Rates
  • By law, we only charge what it costs. No profits are made, and funds cannot be used for other City services.
  • Secured a $37M low-interest loan to spread costs over time. Future residents will also help pay for these 50-year upgrades.
  • We will remain at the lowest combined rate in the region even after the adjustments.

🛡️ Safety & Environment

Safety and Environment
  • Planning state-required upgrades to treat drinking water for Chromium-6.
  • Treating wastewater to state and federal standards to safely release into Monterey Bay or recycle for local farms.
  • Improving trash/recycling to meet state organic waste laws.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why are rate adjustments needed right now? +
Our wastewater treatment plant was built in 1961. The equipment is simply worn out. At the same time, inflation has driven up the cost of the chemicals, fuel, and pipes we need to run our utilities. We must update our rates to cover these true costs of service and replace failing equipment before it breaks.
What happens if the City does not increase rates? +
Delaying repairs costs our customers more money in the long run. For example, a sewer replacement project that cost $15 million in 2021 now costs $37 million today. Doing nothing also forces the City to pay for expensive emergency repairs and puts us at risk of state environmental fines.
Are you using my utility money to pay for other City projects? +
No. By state law, utility funds are strictly protected. Money collected for water, sewer, and trash can only be used to run and repair those specific services. Utility money cannot be used to pay for parks, police, fire, or other general City services.
What is the City doing to keep my bill as low as possible? +
The City works hard to secure outside funding, so current customers do not carry the entire burden. We recently secured a $37 million low-interest state loan to spread the cost of these 50-year upgrades over time. We are also upgrading our waste-to-energy system so we can buy electricity from PG&E at a lower cost.
How do Watsonville's rates compare to neighboring cities? +
Even with the proposed adjustments, Watsonville customers will continue to pay the lowest combined utility rates in our entire region.
Why are single-family and multi-family residential units charged the same sewer rate? +
The City's wastewater rate structure charges all residential customers (single-family and multi-family) a flat monthly rate per dwelling unit. This approach was recommended because wastewater flows are relatively similar across residential dwelling unit types. A charge per dwelling unit reasonably reflects each customer's share of system costs, which are largely fixed (e.g., collection system costs, system maintenance, billing, and administration).
Is there help available for customers who cannot pay their bills? +
If you are having trouble paying your utility bill, please contact our billing office at (831) 768-3133 for a list of outside resources and alternative payment options.
How do I submit a formal protest and/or objection? +

If you own property or are a customer of record, you have the right to submit a written protest against/objection to the proposed rates.

  • Protests must be submitted in writing, include your name and the service address, and received by the City Clerk before the close of the Public Hearing on May 26, 2026. Please read the Notice of Public Hearing document below for exact mailing instructions.
  • Objections must be submitted in writing and include a statement that your communication is a written objection, a description of the nature of the objection, and other important details, which are detailed in the Proposition 218 notice. The deadline for objections is May 20, 2026.
If I live at a property with several separate individual homes but one owner, can we all protest the proposed rates? +
No, only one protest is allowed per parcel.
When will the proposed new rates go into effect? +
If approved by the City Council, the proposed new rates will go into effect on July 1, 2026.
